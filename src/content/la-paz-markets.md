La Paz’s markets entered the recorded route twice, in two different parts of the city and on two different days.

The **Witches’ Market** followed Plaza Murillo on **16 September 2026**, before the route moved into Mi Teleférico. **Mercado Rodríguez** came after the museum sequence on **17 September**, before the afternoon returned to the cable cars and Hernando Siles Stadium.

This is not a guide to every stall, product or ritual associated with either place. The surviving record supports the two stops, their position in the route, and a set of market and street photographs. It does not preserve purchases, prices, opening hours, vendor conversations or a complete inventory. The useful story is therefore the way the markets sit inside the city days: one after the civic centre, the other after the museums.

For the wider discovery view, see the [best things to do in La Paz guide](/best-things-to-do-in-la-paz/), which places the markets alongside Mi Teleférico, Valle de la Luna, civic La Paz and the museum route.

## Why markets became part of the La Paz route

The markets were not isolated add-ons to the city plan. On the first day, the route moved from the civic centre into market streets and then upwards into the cable-car network. On the second, a run of museums and formal buildings gave way to Mercado Rodríguez before the route rose above the streets again.

That makes the markets useful itinerary anchors. They interrupt the sequence of plazas, collections and viewpoints with a more ordinary urban register: stalls, vendors, produce and movement through the centre. The photographs support that broad description. They do not support a more detailed account of what was bought, eaten or discussed.

## The Witches’ Market

The Witches’ Market was the second recorded stop on **16 September**, after Plaza Murillo and before Mi Teleférico. The route window is **3:08–4:08pm**.

What survives is a market-and-street sequence rather than a narrated visit. The archive shows the route entering a dense central area, with colourful shopfronts, stalls and street commerce. That is enough to place the stop in the day and to describe its visual character at a broad level.

It is not enough to reconstruct a complete catalogue of goods or to claim a particular encounter. No purchase, conversation, ritual explanation or named vendor is recorded here, so I have left those familiar guidebook details out. The evidence is stronger when the market is treated as part of the transition from Plaza Murillo to the cable cars, not as a staged anecdote.

For the wider sequence, the [three-day La Paz itinerary](/3-days-in-la-paz-itinerary/) places the market alongside Plaza Murillo, Mi Teleférico and Mirador Killi Killi. The separate [Mi Teleférico in La Paz guide](/mi-teleferico-la-paz/) follows the next part of that route on its own terms.

## Mercado Rodríguez

Mercado Rodríguez appears on **17 September**, from **3:20–4:20pm**, after the day’s museums and before further Mi Teleférico movements and the visit to Hernando Siles Stadium.

The source record describes this stop through its street-level material: vendors, produce and the movement of a working market. That gives it a different role in the itinerary from the museums earlier in the day. The contrast is an editorial reading of the documented route, not a claim that the market can be reduced to a single atmosphere or purpose.

The photographs are deliberately left at that level. They do not establish a full list of products, a specific meal, prices or the boundaries of the market. If you visit, the practical details should be checked separately; they were not preserved in this travel record.

## Two markets, two positions in the day

The most reliable comparison is chronological.

The Witches’ Market came directly after the civic centre, when the first La Paz day was moving from Plaza Murillo into the dense central streets. Mercado Rodríguez came after several museums, when the second day had already spent hours with formal collections and buildings.

Neither stop needs a universal ranking. In this itinerary, each market did a different job: the first opened the route from civic La Paz into the market streets; the second returned the route to vendors, produce and street movement after the museums. That difference is more useful than declaring one “better”.

## How to fit the markets into three days

The market sequence works best when kept inside the two days where it was actually recorded:

1. **16 September:** arrive in La Paz, go to Plaza Murillo, continue to the Witches’ Market, then use the recorded Mi Teleférico and Killi Killi sequence.
2. **17 September:** visit the recorded museums and central buildings, continue to Mercado Rodríguez, then return to the cable cars and Hernando Siles Stadium.
3. **18 September:** keep the separate Valle de la Luna morning and the unresolved afternoon route block rather than moving either market into that day.

The full route is in the [3 Days in La Paz itinerary](/3-days-in-la-paz-itinerary/). The market article is the narrower companion: it explains where the two stops sit in the chronology, while leaving current prices, opening arrangements, transport fares and safety advice to up-to-date checking before travel.

## What this record can and cannot tell you

The evidence is sufficient to say that both markets were visited and to place them accurately in the recorded route. It is not sufficient to claim what a traveller will find on a particular day, how busy either market will be, what a stall will cost, or whether a specific product or service will be available.

That distinction matters here. La Paz is a city of layers, but the archive does not make every layer equally legible. The markets are clearest as lived route markers: one between the civic centre and the cable cars, one between the museums and the city above.
