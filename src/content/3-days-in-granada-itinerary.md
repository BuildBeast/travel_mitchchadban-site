Granada is a city that keeps changing scale. The Alhambra occupies the hill above it, the Albaicín faces it from the opposite slope, and the centre fills the space between with churches, plazas and streets that ask to be crossed slowly.

This guide is built from a documented Granada visit that was made as a day trip from Seville, not from an invented three-night stay. The Alhambra, Generalife, Albaicín and historic-centre material is first-hand; the three-day shape below is an honest way to turn that evidence into a longer Granada itinerary without pretending that every day was travelled in this exact order.

## Granada in three days at a glance

**Day 1 — The Alhambra and Generalife**  
Timed palace visit · Alcazaba · Nasrid Palaces · Generalife gardens

**Day 2 — Albaicín and the view back**  
Albaicín · Mirador de San Nicolás · hill streets · an unhurried return towards the centre

**Day 3 — Granada at street level**  
Cathedral quarter · plazas · historic interiors · a final walk through the city

The recorded source route placed the Alhambra and Albaicín on the same long day from Seville. Separating them here makes the itinerary more usable for a visitor sleeping in Granada, while keeping the distinction between what was personally recorded and what is a practical arrangement.

The Alhambra is the day’s anchor. The surviving account records an early train from Sevilla-Santa Justa, pre-booked guided entry and a long visit to the hilltop complex. It also records the practical point that matters most: the Nasrid Palaces were booked in advance.

Allow the complex its own day. The recorded visit moved through the **Alcazaba**, the **Nasrid Palaces** and the **Generalife** gardens. The architecture is not a single façade but a sequence of courtyards, water, carved surfaces, planted spaces and views opening back over Granada.

### Booking the Alhambra

The recorded trip treated advance booking as essential. That is useful planning evidence, not a promise about current availability or ticket rules. Check the official Alhambra system before travel, and do not build a Granada day around an unconfirmed entry time.

The second day changes the direction of the view. The **Albaicín** faces the Alhambra across the valley, and the recorded route spent its afternoon in the old Moorish quarter before reaching the **Mirador de San Nicolás**.

This is the day to leave room between places. The evidence supports the quarter, the viewpoint and the broad shape of the walk; it does not support a precise sequence of lanes, a crowd forecast or a measured walking time. Treat the route as a framework and let the hill determine the order.

The viewpoint is often described as a compulsory stop. Here it is more useful as a reversal: after a day looking out from the Alhambra, Granada is now the subject of the view.

The third day is deliberately less prescriptive. The source photographs and the recorded visit support a city of plazas, churches, historic façades and interiors, but not a fully evidenced hour-by-hour route or a list of current opening times.

Start around the cathedral quarter and keep the day at street level. The route can take in the cathedral exterior, nearby plazas and the historic centre before returning to whichever part of the city the first two days left unfinished. The point is not to add a second list of must-sees. It is to give the city below the Alhambra enough time to become more than a transfer between viewpoints.

## Practical notes

### If you are coming from Seville

The recorded journey departed from **Sevilla-Santa Justa** and treated Granada as a long day trip. The legacy account records roughly three and a half hours by rail, an early departure and a return around 18:55. Those are trip-record details, not a current timetable, fare or guarantee; check the live rail schedule before booking.

The first-hand day-trip account is not a substitute for choosing where to sleep. If Granada is the purpose of the trip, a local base gives the Alhambra and Albaicín separate days instead of compressing both into one return journey.

### What this article does not claim

It does not claim a particular hotel in Granada, a current ticket price, an opening hour, a queue length, a restaurant recommendation or a current transport fare. Those details need checking close to travel. It also avoids assigning exact dates to the photographs: the archive is useful for the places shown, but the article does not rely on a resolved camera-clock sequence.

## The shape of the city

Three days in Granada work because the city can be read in layers. The Alhambra supplies the high view and the architectural centre of gravity. The Albaicín turns that view around. The third day returns to the streets, where Granada is less a monument than a sequence of thresholds between hill, palace, church and plaza.

That is the useful itinerary: not a claim to have completed three identical days, but a way to give the recorded experience enough space to be repeated honestly.
