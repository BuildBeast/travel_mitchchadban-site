## Why Buenos Aires makes more sense barrio by barrio

The itinerary crossed several named districts, but the change was not only a matter of geography. Each section brought a different group of recorded places into view. ## Which area stayed with me most?

The answer is not a ranking. Palermo held the stay together because it was the base and the place returned to for parks. Recoleta concentrated a dense cultural route. San Telmo and Puerto Madero made a single day change direction twice. La Boca brought art, structures and football architecture into the final full-day route.

What stayed with me was the sequence itself: the way Buenos Aires became a different city each time the itinerary crossed into another district. That is a first-hand observation about this trip, not a verdict on which barrio a future traveller should choose.

## If I had less time

With less time, I would not try to reproduce every recorded stop. I would choose one central route and one district pairing, then leave room for the places that made the comparison useful. The evidence supports the following shape without turning it into a universal checklist:

1. Begin with the historic centre and its civic landmarks.
2. Add Recoleta if the cemetery, Floralis Genérica and museum route are the priority.
3. Use San Telmo and Puerto Madero together if markets, cultural stops and waterfront space are the draw.
4. Keep La Boca as a separate route section rather than reducing it to the stadium alone.
5. Return to Palermo for the parks and Planetarium sequence recorded at the end of the stay.

This is a way of reading my five-night route, not a claim that one neighbourhood is objectively better than another.
