Lima does not reveal itself in a single direction.

There is the ceremonial city of plazas, churches and government buildings. There is Barranco, painted and low-rise, connected by lanes, parks and public spaces. There is Miraflores along the cliffs. There are museums full of earlier versions of Peru, and archaeological sites sitting inside modern neighbourhoods as the city carries on around them.

Three days was enough to see all of those Limas.

Not slowly.

The route was ambitious from the beginning: arrive from Mexico, complete the accommodation handoff in Jesús María, and begin sightseeing in the historic centre that same afternoon. The following day crossed Barranco and Miraflores before turning inland through archaeological sites and museums. The third returned to central Lima for another museum-heavy day before departure toward Cusco on 13 September.

This is not a prescription for a relaxed weekend.

For a discovery-led shortlist of the experiences behind this route, start with the [best things to do in Lima guide](/best-things-to-do-in-lima/).

And it is a useful demonstration of how much city can sit inside Lima.

The geography matters. This itinerary does not unfold as one neat walking loop. It uses walking within neighbourhood clusters and short drives between districts. That is part of the character of the trip.

The second day is the heart of this itinerary.

It is also the day to divide if the trip has four days rather than three.

The route began in **Barranco**, where several stops formed a walkable cluster: **El Cacaotal**, the **Bridge of Sighs**, the **Bajada de Baños**, **Barranco Municipal Park**, **Museo Mario Testino** and **Museo Pedro de Osma**.

The short walk from El Cacaotal to the Bridge of Sighs was followed by walking between the Barranco landmarks and museums. Barranco works here as a neighbourhood rather than a collection of isolated pins. Streets, public spaces, colourful walls and museums connect naturally enough that moving between them becomes part of the visit.

Until this point, much of the route had been streets, façades, museums and enclosed urban spaces. Then there was the Pacific: a coastal view, cliffside roads and palms replacing the tight geometry of the historic centre.

It is a different visual register from Plaza Mayor, yet still part of the same day and the same city.

The contrast is one reason Lima works well over several days rather than as a single historic-centre visit.

After the coast, the itinerary turned inland.

There was a stop at the **Miraflores Indian Market**, followed by one of the most distinctive sequences of the trip: **Pucllana**, **Huaca Huallamarca** and then the **Larco Museum**.

For a focused account of the archaeological stop inside Miraflores, continue to the [Huaca Pucllana guide](/huaca-pucllana-lima/). This page remains the complete three-day Lima route.

The day continued from Huaca Huallamarca to the **Larco Museum**, before circling back toward Miraflores for dinner at **Restaurant Huaca Pucllana**.

It was an ambitious day: Barranco, the coast, a market, two archaeological sites, Larco and dinner.

The useful lesson is not that every visitor should reproduce it minute by minute. It is that these areas can be combined geographically, while the complete sequence is still demanding.

If you have four days rather than three, this is the day to divide.

## Why Jesús María Worked as a Base

The base for all three nights was **Jesús María**.

That shaped the itinerary. The route travelled outward each day rather than beginning in Miraflores or Barranco, moving between Jesús María, the historic centre, Barranco, Miraflores, San Isidro, Pueblo Libre and central Lima.

For this route, Lima worked less as a single neighbourhood stay and more as a city crossed repeatedly. If choosing accommodation for a first visit, treat the base as a separate planning decision rather than assuming this exact location is necessary to reproduce the itinerary.

## Is Three Days Enough for Lima?

Three days can cover a remarkable amount of Lima. This route included:

- the historic centre
- Barranco
- Miraflores
- the Pacific coast
- archaeological sites
- museums
- a market
- public parks
- multiple neighbourhoods

There is a catch: the itinerary is fast.

Day 2 in particular would be more comfortable split across two days. Three days works if you prefer full days and moving quickly; four days would make the same route substantially gentler.

What three days should not become is an attempt to see every museum, archaeological site and neighbourhood Lima has to offer. The strength of the city is precisely that there is more underneath the obvious route.

Three days in Lima did not produce a single clean portrait of the city.

That is the point.

The historic centre belongs to one Lima. Barranco belongs to another. Miraflores meets the Pacific at the edge of the city. Archaeological sites interrupt the modern streets and reveal another city underneath them.

A short stay cannot resolve those versions into one story.

It can only move between them: from a colonial square to a painted Barranco street, from the cliffs above the Pacific to archaeological ground inside the modern city, and from museums to buses, vendors and public parks.

Lima is not one city hiding behind another.

It is all of them at once.

And three days is just enough time to notice.
