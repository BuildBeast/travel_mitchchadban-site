Lima does not reveal itself in a single direction.

There is the ceremonial city of plazas, churches and government buildings. There is Barranco, painted and low-rise, connected by lanes, parks and public spaces. There is Miraflores along the cliffs. There are museums full of earlier versions of Peru, and archaeological sites sitting inside modern neighbourhoods as the city carries on around them.

Three days was enough to see all of those Limas.

Not slowly.

The route was ambitious from the beginning: arrive from Mexico, complete the accommodation handoff in Jesús María, and begin sightseeing in the historic centre that same afternoon. The following day crossed Barranco and Miraflores before turning inland through archaeological sites and museums. The third returned to central Lima for another museum-heavy day before departure toward Cusco on 13 September.

This is not a prescription for a relaxed weekend.

It is the route recorded in the trip itinerary.

And it is a useful demonstration of how much city can sit inside Lima.

## Where I Stayed: Jesús María

The base for all three nights was **Jesús María**, at Avenida General Eugenio Garzón 550, Dpto 1507.

That shaped the itinerary. The route travelled outward each day rather than beginning in Miraflores or Barranco, with recorded movement between Jesús María, the historic centre, Barranco, Miraflores, San Isidro, Pueblo Libre and central Lima.

The trip records describe the area as residential and quiet at night. That is a planning note from the source material, not a universal neighbourhood assessment.

For this route, Lima worked less as a single neighbourhood stay and more as a city crossed repeatedly. If choosing accommodation for a first visit, treat the base as a separate planning decision rather than assuming this exact location is necessary to reproduce the itinerary.

## Getting Around Lima

The recorded itinerary used a mixture of walking and vehicle transfers.

The useful distinction is where walking makes sense.

Barranco’s first cluster was walked between El Cacaotal, the Bridge of Sighs, the Bajada de Baños, Barranco Park and the two museums. The route also records a 1.6 km walk from Love Park to the Malecón and a 1.9 km walk from Pucllana to Huaca Huallamarca.

Other transitions were treated as drives in the Sortie record, including the 7.6 km journey from Larco to Restaurant Huaca Pucllana.

The source plan recommends Uber or Cabify for airport and accommodation transfers, but the actual provider used is not confirmed. No fares are recorded.

The practical rule from this itinerary is simple:

**walk the neighbourhood; ride between the neighbourhoods.**

That is more realistic than trying to turn the entire three-day route into one continuous walking route.

## Is Three Days Enough for Lima?

Three days can cover a remarkable amount of Lima. This route included:

- the historic centre
- Barranco
- Miraflores
- the Pacific coast
- archaeological sites
- museums
- a market
- public parks
- multiple neighbourhoods

There is a catch: the itinerary is fast.

Day 2 in particular would be more comfortable split across two days. Three days works if you prefer full days and moving quickly; four days would make the same route substantially gentler.

What three days should not become is an attempt to see every museum, archaeological site and neighbourhood Lima has to offer. The strength of the city is precisely that there is more underneath the obvious route.

## A Practical Three-Day Lima Route

### Day 1

Arrive → Jesús María → San Francisco → Plaza Mayor → Cathedral → Government Palace area → Santo Domingo → Las Nazarenas → dinner

### Day 2

Barranco → Bridge of Sighs → Bajada de Baños → Barranco museums → Miraflores → Love Park → Malecón → Pucllana → Huaca Huallamarca → Larco Museum → dinner

### Day 3

Central Lima parks → MALI → Museo de Minerales Andrés del Castillo → INEI → lunch → Kennedy Park → flexible afternoon/evening

The final flexible block is deliberate. The original third day contained an uncertain stadium plan. Rather than replacing it with an invented attraction for the sake of a symmetrical itinerary, leave the afternoon open.

The route already contains more than enough confirmed material.

Three days in Lima did not produce a single clean portrait of the city.

That is the point.

The historic centre belongs to one Lima. Barranco belongs to another. Miraflores meets the Pacific at the edge of the city. Archaeological sites interrupt the modern streets and reveal another city underneath them.

A short stay cannot resolve those versions into one story.

It can only move between them: from a colonial square to a painted Barranco street, from the cliffs above the Pacific to archaeological ground inside the modern city, and from museums to buses, vendors and public parks.

Lima is not one city hiding behind another.

It is all of them at once.

And three days is just enough time to notice.
