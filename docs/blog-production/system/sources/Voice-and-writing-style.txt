THERE AND BACK AGAIN — BLOG WRITING SYSTEM
1. Core Identity (What this blog is)

This is not travel blogging in the typical sense.

It is:

A literary travel chronicle
Part guide, part memoir, part field report
Written as if the world is being reconstructed from memory and receipts
Obsessed with place, food, movement, and atmosphere
Anchored in real logistics (costs, routes, restaurants) but elevated in tone

The underlying idea:

“Every journey is both practical and mythological at the same time.”

2. Voice (How it should sound)
Primary voice: “Reflective literary traveller”
Calm, observational, slightly archaic at times
Confident, not enthusiastic
Never casual or influencer-like
Descriptive without being decorative for decoration’s sake
Tone qualities:
Measured (never rushed)
Sensory (food, weather, architecture, movement)
Slightly epic, but grounded
Occasionally poetic, never sentimental
What to avoid:
No hype (“insane”, “must visit”, “hidden gem” spam)
No influencer voice (“you NEED to go here”)
No over-explaining obvious things
No modern internet slang
3. Narrative Structure (Every post)

Each blog follows a consistent ritual structure:

I. The Arrival (Opening)
Set the journey frame
Time, place, transport arrival detail
Emotional state without stating emotions directly
Establish “this is a passage through space”

Think: train station / airport / ferry / border crossing

II. The World Unfolds (City Sections / Chapters)

Each location is treated like a chaptered mythological place

Each chapter contains:

A name + epithet (e.g. “Rome — The Eternal”)
A short weather / seasonal badge
2–4 paragraphs of narrative prose:
movement through the city
meals as anchors
sensory detail
historical or cultural texture (light touch)

Important:

Food is not a recommendation list here — it is story structure
Places are introduced through experience, not description dumps
III. Practical Layer (Structured Breakdown)

This is your “dual-layer system”:

After narrative:

“Where to Eat”
“Getting There”
“Budget Snapshot”
“Tips & Watch-outs”

This section is:

factual
scannable
useful
not poetic

Think:

“The map after the story”

IV. Beat Lines / Transitions

You use these as structural breathing points:

“But the road is ever onward.”

These should:

reset tone
signal movement between cities
prevent fatigue
feel slightly mythic
V. Closing Reflection (Optional but powerful)

Not every post needs one, but when used:

very restrained
no moral conclusion
no “lesson learned”
just observation or implication of continuation
4. Language Rules
Sentence style
Medium-to-long sentences preferred
Occasional long, flowing sentences with internal rhythm
Avoid punchy TikTok-style phrasing
Nouns over adjectives

Prefer:

“the porticoes of Bologna”
over
“beautiful old covered walkways”
Time handling

Time is always:

specific (dates, trains, arrivals)
but never overly technical unless needed
5. Food Philosophy (Very important in your project)

Food is treated as:

cultural anchor
narrative device
memory trigger

Not:

“restaurant recommendations content”

Each food moment should:

appear inside story first
then reappear in practical section

Rule:

“If it appears in the prose, it must exist in the practical section.”

6. Character of the Traveller

The narrator is:

unnamed or lightly defined (“the traveller”)
observant rather than expressive
often slightly removed from events
occasionally self-aware but never self-indulgent

They are:

A recorder of places, not the centre of them

7. Structural Aesthetic Rules (matches your CSS system)

Your writing must align with the visual system:

Chapters = visual hierarchy (like monuments)
Breaks = spacing, silence, restraint
Pull quotes = philosophical or sensory fragments
Stats / budget = grounding mechanism
Lists = controlled, minimal, functional

Think:

“Typography is narrative pacing”

8. SEO Layer (subtle, not dominant)

Each post should naturally include:

real city names
real restaurant names
transport routes
geographic flow

But:

never keyword-stuffed
never written for algorithms first

Meta description logic:

“One sentence: route + tone + authenticity signal”

Example style:

“A 30-day journey from Bologna through Rome and Morocco into Andalucía, blending real routes, restaurants, and costs with narrative travel writing.”
9. Core Writing Formula (use this when stuck)

For any section:

1. Movement

Where is the traveller physically?

2. Detail

What is one specific sensory anchor?

3. Place truth

What makes this place itself (not generic travel description)?

4. Slight reflection

What does this moment feel like in context of journey?

10. Golden Rule of the Project

If everything else is forgotten, keep this:

“This is travel writing that refuses to become content.”
