## Purpose

Answer: **what is the strongest realistic Seville route for one complete day?** The editorial content is the *subtraction* — what one day cannot hold, and why. This must not read as a compressed `/3-days-in-seville/`.

## Key decisions

1. **The route opens at Plaza de España at about 08:15, before the Alcázar gate opens.** This is the article's central move and the reason it is not Day 1 of the three-day route. Plaza de España is free, unenclosed and has no opening hour **[PRIMARY — carried from the 3-day packet]**; the Alcázar's first timed slot is 09:30 **[PRIMARY]**. So the one hour the three-day route can afford to spend anywhere is, at one day, the *only* hour that is free — and it is spent on the one major sight that costs nothing and needs no ticket. It also solves the heat problem before it starts.
2. **Four ticketed or timed things, maximum.** Alcázar, Cathedral + Giralda, Archivo de Indias (free), and one evening choice. Everything else is walking.
3. **One variation only: Sunday.** Chosen over Monday because the Sunday constraint is harder (the Cathedral does not open to visitors until 14:30 outside July–August **[PRIMARY]**), it affects more readers, and the Monday problem at one day is minor — the Archivo closes, and the Archivo is the one droppable item. The Monday case is handled in two sentences inside the Sunday section rather than given a second route.
4. **Explicitly refuse the greedy day.** The brief's list — Alcázar, Cathedral, Giralda, Santa Cruz, Triana, Plaza de España, Parque de María Luisa, the Setas, several museums — is named as a thing the article will not pretend is possible, with the arithmetic shown.
5. **The evening is a single choice, made for the reader.** The Setas over Triana, because it is ten minutes from Santa Cruz's northern edge and gives back the ground already walked, whereas Triana adds a river crossing and a neighbourhood the day has no time to earn. Triana is named as the alternative for a reader who would rather end by water.

## Geographic logic

A single north–south line, walked once, with no doubling back except the 12-minute leg from the plaza to the Alcázar gate:

`Plaza de España (south) → Alcázar → Cathedral → Archivo (all within 250 m) → Santa Cruz (5 min east) → Setas (10 min north)`

The whole day fits inside a corridor about 1.5 km long. **This is why one day in Seville works at all** — and why adding Triana, which sits off the corridor across a bridge, is the specific thing that breaks it.
