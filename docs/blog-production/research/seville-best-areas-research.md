# Research Note — Best Areas to Stay in Seville

**Provenance key:** **[REPO]** existing repository content · **[PHOTO]** Mitch's photographs + EXIF · **[PRIMARY]** official page opened and read · **[SECONDARY]** reputable specialist source, used only where no official page answers the question · **[INFERENCE]** my reasoning, labelled

## Purpose

Answer one decision: **which Seville neighbourhood should this particular traveller book?** The page is a comparative assessment of six areas, not a hotel directory and not an itinerary. It is the only page in the cluster permitted to argue about accommodation.

## Key decisions

1. **Six areas, not ten.** Santa Cruz, El Arenal, Centro/Alfalfa, San Lorenzo–Alameda, Triana, and La Macarena. Los Remedios and Nervión were considered and cut: neither adds a *distinct choice* for a first-time visitor — both are "cheaper, further, residential", which is one answer, not two. Nervión is named once inside the Macarena/edge discussion rather than given its own section.
2. **San Lorenzo and Alameda are treated as one area with two characters**, because they adjoin and the practical difference is noise at 01:00, not geography. This is honest about the single documented stay: **[REPO]** places the accommodation "in a street behind San Lorenzo", on Calle Hombre de Piedra, *on the edge of* the Alameda bar strip.
3. **Structure per area:** atmosphere · distance to the monumental core · walking convenience · evening character · noise · heat and shade · transport · likely accommodation character · who it suits · who should avoid it · the single trade-off. Then a decision table, then the questions that actually decide it (noise, heat, luggage, length of stay).
4. **No hotel or apartment names, no per-night prices.** Only one street is documented and the only recorded cost is a trip-level AUD figure. Naming a property would be invention; quoting euro nightly rates would be fabrication.
5. **No affiliate links.**

## Geographic logic

The page is organised **by distance from the monumental core**, inside-out, because that is the axis the reader is actually trading against:

1. **Santa Cruz** — inside the core. 0–5 min to the Cathedral.
2. **El Arenal** — west of the core, on the river. 5–10 min.
3. **Centro / Alfalfa** — north of the core. 5–12 min.
4. **San Lorenzo / Alameda** — north-west, outside the core. 15–20 min.
5. **Triana** — across the Guadalquivir. 20–25 min plus a bridge.
6. **La Macarena** — north, furthest. 20–30 min.

The organising insight: in a city where every central sight sits inside a 2 km oval, **proximity stops being the variable that matters at about the fifteen-minute mark**, and noise, shade and price take over. That is the page's argument and it is what stops it becoming a distance table.
