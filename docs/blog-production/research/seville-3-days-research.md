## 14. Stable facts (safe to write; low refresh risk)

- Seville's monumental core — Cathedral, Giralda, Real Alcázar, Archivo de Indias, Plaza del Triunfo — sits within roughly **400 m** of a single point; the Archivo faces the Cathedral across the plaza
- The Alcázar has been continuously inhabited since the 12th century; the Christian kings extended rather than replaced the Moorish palace **[REPO]**
- The Giralda is the converted Almohad minaret of the former mosque and is ascended by **ramps, not stairs**
- Plaza de España and Parque María Luisa were built for the **1929 Ibero-American Exposition**; Plaza de España is a semicircle with a canal, four bridges and tiled alcoves for the Spanish provinces **[REPO]**
- Triana lies on the **west bank** of the Guadalquivir, reached from the centre by the **Puente de Isabel II**
- The Torre del Oro was built by the Almohads in **1221** **[REPO]**
- The Archivo de Indias holds the Spanish colonial archive on ~8 km of shelving **[REPO]**
- The Setas de Sevilla (Metropol Parasol) stands in Plaza de la Encarnación and carries a rooftop walkway
- Casa de Pilatos is a 16th-century palace on Plaza de Pilatos, a short walk east of the Cathedral **[REPO]**
- **Seville regularly exceeds 40 °C in July and August** — stated in the site's own FAQ **[REPO]** and the reason the midday-gap advice exists
- The old city is walkable end to end; a 3-day itinerary needs no public transport **[REPO]**

## 19. Geographic route analysis

Distances are straight-line/street estimates from map geometry, rounded, and marked **[INFERENCE]**. Every one should be sanity-checked on a map at draft stage.

### The four spatial clusters of central Seville

1. **The monumental core** — Cathedral, Giralda, Alcázar, Archivo de Indias, Plaza del Triunfo, Santa Cruz. A radius of roughly **250 m**. The densest concentration of major sights in the city
2. **The southern park axis** — Plaza de España, Parque María Luisa. Begins ~500 m south of the Alcázar and runs a further ~1 km south. Contiguous with the Alcázar's southern edge
3. **The river line** — Torre del Oro and Maestranza on the east bank; Triana and Calle Betis across the Puente de Isabel II. Runs roughly north–south along the water, ~1.5 km end to end
4. **The northern old city** — Casa de Pilatos (east-centre), Plaza de la Encarnación / Setas, Alameda de Hércules, San Lorenzo, Macarena. Spread across ~1.5 km

### Why the pilot file's hypothesis is rejected

The pilot proposed: *D1 monumental core + Santa Cruz · D2 Triana, river, food · D3 northern/alternative Seville, parks, or a slower thematic day.*

Day 1 is sound. **Days 2 and 3 are not**, for one decisive reason: the hypothesis parks **Plaza de España and Parque María Luisa in a "northern Seville" day**. Those sights are in the far **south**. Pairing them with Macarena or the Alameda forces a ~2.5 km diagonal traverse of the entire old city, twice, in the middle of the day. It also strands Seville's second-most-photographed sight in a leftovers day, and leaves Day 3 the weakest of the three — which the brief specifically asks to test for.

The hypothesis also mislocates the market: **Mercado de Triana is a morning institution**, but the hypothesis's Day 2 treats Triana as an afternoon/evening river day.

**The structure is therefore replaced, not preserved.**

### Recommended structure

#### Day 1 — The Monumental Core *(radial; almost zero transit)*

`Real Alcázar (09:30 timed entry)` → `Cathedral & Giralda` → `Archivo de Indias` → *late lunch* → `Barrio de Santa Cruz` in the shaded late afternoon → *evening tapas, Santa Cruz / Alfalfa*

- **Total walking: well under 2 km.** Every transition is 3–6 minutes
- Puts the one item that genuinely sells out at the very start of the trip
- The Archivo is free and air-conditioned — a deliberate placement in the hottest part of the day
- Santa Cruz's lanes are narrow and shaded: correct for late afternoon, poor at noon
- **Weekday rule:** not a Sunday (Cathedral opens 14:30); not a Monday **if** the Archivo Monday closure is confirmed

#### Day 2 — The River, Triana and the Bullring *(linear; one bridge)*

`Mercado de Triana (early)` → `Centro Cerámica Triana` → `Calle Betis / the west bank` → *cross Puente de Isabel II* → `Torre del Oro` → `Plaza de Toros de la Maestranza` → *afternoon break* → *evening: Calle Betis at sunset, or flamenco*

- **Total walking: ~3 km, linear**, no backtracking
- Market first because it is a morning market
- Puts the reader on the west bank in the morning and back on the east bank by afternoon, with the river as the spine
- Returns to Triana for the evening — the one deliberate repeat, and it is justified: Calle Betis at sunset is a different place from Calle Betis at 09:00
- **Weekday rule:** not a Sunday **if** the Mercado closure is confirmed

#### Day 3 — South to North: Parks, Palace, Parasol *(linear traverse)*

`Plaza de España (early, ~08:30–09:30)` → `Parque María Luisa` → *walk north* → `Casa de Pilatos` → *lunch + break* → `Setas de Sevilla at dusk`

- **Total walking: ~3.5 km, one continuous south-to-north line**
- **Plaza de España first thing is the strongest single piece of advice in the article.** It is open, unenclosed and free; before ~09:30 it is nearly empty, the light is low across the tiles, and the heat has not arrived. By noon it is the most crowded and most exposed place in Seville
- Casa de Pilatos is open every day 09:00–18:00 — the flexible anchor that lets this day absorb a Sunday or Monday
- Ends at the Setas at dusk: the highest accessible viewpoint, the city's rooftops, and — subject to §15 — a way to end on the Giralda without depending on the belfry works
- **Weekday rule:** works on any day. **This is the day to schedule on a Sunday or Monday.**

### The scheduling rule (a genuine reader takeaway)

> Day 3 is the flexible one. If your three days include a Sunday or a Monday, put **Day 3** on it. Day 1 needs the Cathedral open in the morning; Day 2 needs the market open.

## 22. Seasonal limitations

- **July–August:** Seville regularly exceeds 40 °C **[REPO]**. Any 3-day itinerary in these months must compress into ~08:30–13:00 and ~18:30–22:00. The route above holds, but the afternoon break becomes non-negotiable
- **Also in 2026:** the Cathedral's summer schedule is disrupted by the Giralda works **[PRIMARY — P3]**, and 4–23 August it does not open until 11:00
- **Late September – October:** 28–32 °C, thinner crowds **[REPO]**. Alcázar summer hours run to 30 September; from 1 October it closes at 17:00 **[PRIMARY]**, which compresses Day 1
- **April–May:** strong, but **Semana Santa and the Feria de Abril** bring closures, route disruption and much higher prices. **No supporting material exists — write about them only in general, sourced terms, or not at all**
- **November–March:** Alcázar closes 17:00; short daylight moves the Day 3 dusk finish to ~18:00

## 23. Heat-related route considerations

The route is built around the heat, not decorated with a warning about it:

- **Day 1** places the free, air-conditioned Archivo de Indias in the early-afternoon peak, and Santa Cruz's shaded lanes in the late afternoon
- **Day 2** puts the covered market in the morning and defers Calle Betis to sunset
- **Day 3** front-loads Plaza de España — the largest unshaded open space on the route — into the coolest hour of the day, and ends on a rooftop after dark
- All three days assume a **13:30–16:30 break**. **[PHOTO]** independently corroborates this rhythm: on 29 August 2023 there is a clean gap in the record between 13:43 and 16:25
- Parque María Luisa is genuinely shaded and is the correct refuge on Day 3

## 24. Accessibility limitations

To be stated plainly and verified before publication:

- **The Giralda has no lift.** The ascent is by 35 ramps and a short flight of steps
- **Santa Cruz** is narrow, cobbled and uneven
- **Calle Betis** and the Triana riverbank involve steps and slopes at several access points
- **The Setas** rooftop walkway is reached by lift, but the walkway itself undulates
- The **Alcázar** and **Archivo de Indias** are largely step-free on their main routes; the Alcázar's **Cuarto Real Alto** is not
- **[UNVERIFIED]** — every one of these needs confirmation against each venue's own accessibility page before it is published. Do not publish an accessibility claim on inference.
