## Purpose

Answer: **which Seville experiences deserve priority, and how should a traveller choose among them?** Not a flat ranked list. Every entry carries a verdict, including a skip-verdict, and a first-visit / longer-stay allocation.

## Key decisions

1. **Sixteen entries, in seven thematic groups.** Sixteen because that is the number that survived the test *"can I say something specific about why this is worth a stranger's afternoon?"* — not because sixteen is a good headline. The article says so.
2. **Groups are geographic-and-thematic at once**, so the grouping does double duty as pairing advice: Monumental Seville · The River and the Trade · The Private Palaces · Parks and Open Ground · Art, Tile and What the Convents Left · Seville After Dark · The Ones That Need Booking (a cross-cutting summary, not a group of new entries).
3. **Every entry has a fixed six-part spine:** why it matters · what makes it *Seville* rather than generic · time required · what it pairs with geographically · booking requirement · who can reasonably skip it, and whether it is a first visit or a longer stay.
4. **No day-by-day ordering anywhere.** That is the route pages' job. The pairing lines give geography without emitting an itinerary.
5. **Includes sights no route on this site covers** — Museo de Bellas Artes, Palacio de las Dueñas, Condesa de Lebrija, Basílica de la Macarena, Hospital de los Venerables, the Antiquarium beneath the Setas, Isla Mágica — which is a large part of what stops this page duplicating `/3-days-in-seville/`.
6. **Flamenco is included and treated as an entry, not an add-on**, because the three-day page explicitly excludes it as "not a sight". Here it is assessed properly: tablao versus dinner show versus museum performance.
7. **No FAQ section**, therefore no FAQPage schema.

## Refresh-sensitive facts and phrasing

- **Torre del Oro hours are not quoted.** Sources disagree; only the ~€3 admission and the free-Monday convention are given, both flagged as worth confirming.
- **Museo de Bellas Artes**: the "free to EU citizens" rule is the durable, decision-relevant fact and leads; the exact hours are given with the August variation and a check instruction.
- **Maestranza**: the *bullfight-day* short schedule is the fact that actually ruins visits, so it leads; the price is not quoted.
- **Cathedral / Giralda**: no opening time asserted; works flagged with no predicted end.
- **Palace hours** carry season splits, given as seasons rather than a single line.
- **Flamenco**: no venue named, no price quoted. The article gives the *categories* (tablao, dinner show, museum performance) and how to tell them apart, which does not go stale.
- **Prices** appear once each, in practical blocks, behind a verified-on date.

## Geographic logic

Pairing lines are derived from the four spatial clusters established in the 3-day packet: the monumental core (~250 m radius), the southern park axis, the river line, and the northern old city. Every entry names the cluster it belongs to, so a reader can build their own day without the page building one for them. Two entries deliberately cross clusters — the river (which is the axis, not a point) and flamenco (which is an hour, not a place).
